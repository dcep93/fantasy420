# All-years Wrapped views and competitive matchup spice

Approved by the user's yesi, with MondayNight added to the removal list.

Remove MondayNight, StandingsPrediction, StdDev, and Punts from navigation. Offer All years in the shared year selector. Include the scope in the page heading and preserve it in the URL. Invalid years fall back to the current season. Season-only views explain why they require a season and offer a season selection rather than silently showing different data.

Aggregate meaningful historical views using season-local calculations and year/week identities. Do not merge raw snapshots, rename week numbers, or mutate global selection during rendering. Shared aggregate records support manager totals/rates, head-to-head records, weekly extremes, lineup decisions, started/benched points, player distributions, unique players, ownership history, position scoring, player-season draft rankings, and inferred ownership moves. Recalculate rates from pooled observations, distinguish ties, omit unfinished weeks and week zero, and distinguish manager eras where team IDs were reused. Event leaderboards pool all seasons before ranking. Retain year/week and season-specific team names on events. Tables expose their metric and sample counts and allow searching/sorting for useful large results; manager trends and plots retain chart representations.

All-years support: ManagerPlot, PlayerPlot, PlayerStats, WeekTopsAndBottoms, SqueezesAndStomps, GooseEggs, Bopped, ChosenWrong, DraftValue, ReachesAndSteals, Performance, PerformanceTotals, ManagerTrend, DeterminedByDiscreteScoring, Stacks, Negatives, PointsAgainst, UniquesStarted, UniquesRostered, BoomBust, Benchwarmers, Injuries, BestByPosition, AllTimeRecords, HeadToHead, SecondLost, WhatIf, ConsistentlyAverage, Trades, Simps, PositionTrends, SpiciestMatchups, json.

These views do not have a coherent additive interpretation without changing their question.

PlayerStats and PositionTrends use their existing NFL historical dataset, including years before the league existed, and clearly label their actual coverage. Numeric selection filters history instead of ignoring it. Existing intrinsically historical league views also respect numeric selection.

SpiciestMatchups fetches each season separately, computes its matchups, and globally sorts the results. Partial fetch failures name excluded years; no fake zero scores are introduced. Correct suffix matching (Jr, Sr, II, III, IV, V) to match the actual surname, including abbreviated first names. Still label the reconstruction honestly: final points are distributed over mentions, not scored from actual play statistics.

Spice uses directional swings of at least five points, but rewards only comebacks toward a competitive margin or reversals across zero. Extending an existing lead earns no swing points. A comeback ending more than ten points from a tie earns zero; a comeback ending within ten receives linearly weighted credit, and crossing zero receives credit for the recovered deficit plus at most ten points of the new lead. This keeps material comebacks while suppressing harmless movement within blowouts. Sum the top three qualifying swings; retain capped final-slot flip bonuses. Tiny early wiggles do not count. Display competitive-swing wording and the exact explanation.
