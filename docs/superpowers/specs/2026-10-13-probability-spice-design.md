# Probability-based matchup spice

## Intent and authorization
The user found 2026 rank 10 dull and authorized choosing and implementing the best improvement. Rank 10 never changes the favorite; 92% of its old score comes from shrinking a large point deficit. Rank the suspense visible in the existing probability chart. Work on main, preserve unrelated dist output, commit and push the completed change.

## Alternatives and decision
Keeping point swings with a smaller comeback multiplier would preserve the mismatch with the chart. Counting every 50% crossing would reward reconstructed noise. Use competitive probability swings, decisive reversals, and late suspense instead, with an explicit minimum score so ten is a maximum, not a quota.

## Scoring contract
Use the existing headToHeadProbability on each raw timeline point (actual probability, never the square-root display coordinate). All components are symmetric when teams swap. Round individual swing credits and components to two decimals before summing, so displayed credits add to their subtotal and displayed components add to the total.

1. Competitive probability swings: retain peak/trough segmentation, but require a 10 percentage-point reversal to split a swing. Work in percentage points relative to 50. Ignore moves away from a tie. For a swing crossing 50%, credit the recovered probability deficit plus at most 10 percentage points beyond 50%. For one stopping short, multiply its size by max(0, 1 - distance from 50 / 10). Sum the three largest positive credits.
2. Decisive reversals: establish a favorite only at 60% or above (opponent at 40% or below). Count a reversal only after the opposite team reaches 60%; ties and intermediate probabilities preserve the established favorite. First acquisition earns no bonus. Each reversal earns 5 spice, doubled to 10 in the final 25% of projected scoring remaining. Cap this component at 30. Store total and late reversal counts and the last reversal time for explanation. Interpolate the 40/60 threshold crossing and remaining work across continuous intervals; treat a 1 ms scoring jump as occurring at its endpoint. This keeps extra linear samples from changing the late classification.
3. Late suspense: remaining work at a point is sum(max(0, projectedScore - earnedScore)) for both teams. Divide by its initial value for the fraction remaining. Integrate max(0, 1 - abs(p - 50) / 25) over remaining-work fractions from 0.25 down to 0, normalize by 0.25, and multiply by 20. Use piecewise-linear probability between samples and split at the 25%, 50%, 75% probability boundaries for exact triangular integration. Duplicate points and idle elapsed time add nothing. Zero initial remaining work earns no suspense and no late multiplier. Clamp final component to 0..20.
4. Qualification: show up to ten matchups with total score >=25. Keep computing all eligible completed games for analysis; apply qualification only in the presentation loader. Explain the threshold and show a distinct no-qualifying-matchups state. Preserve missing projection and failed season messages. Do not pad the list or penalize genuine reversals merely because their final score becomes a blowout.
